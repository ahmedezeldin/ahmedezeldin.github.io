Project

Medicine Cooler

Team engineering design project · University of Lethbridge (ENGG 1600)

A four-person first-year design project: a smart cooler to keep temperature-sensitive medication such as insulin within a 2–8°C target range. I wrote the ESP32 firmware and designed the enclosure.

How it works · 3D render1 / 5

  1. The finished design

    A 3D render of the completed cooler: both printed halves bonded together, wiring routed out of sight, and the control unit mounted to the side. The photos below show the working prototype.

  2. Insulated enclosure

    Seven 3D-printed parts — insulated outer shell, inner liner, electronics bay and lids — with an insulation layer between the shell and the liner that holds the medication.

  3. Peltier cooling stage

    A thermoelectric (Peltier) module pulls heat out of the liner, and the heat sink and fan release it outside. A relay switches the stage on and off.

  4. Electronics bay

    An ESP32 reads the DS18B20 temperature probe, shows live readings on a 128×64 OLED, drives the cooling relay, and sounds the buzzer.

  5. Control loop

    The firmware compares each reading with the 2–8°C target: the relay runs the cooling stage while it is too warm, and the buzzer alert escalates if it stays out of range. (Values shown are illustrative.)

My contributions
